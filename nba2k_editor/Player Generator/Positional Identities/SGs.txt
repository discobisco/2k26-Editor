How to read this (SG version)

A “shooting guard archetype” is best treated as a stack of roles, not a single bucket. A two‑guard can contribute via:

Primary scoring mode (spot-up, movement, pull-up, rim pressure, post, etc.)

Off-ball value (relocation, screening, cutting, gravity/decoy)

Secondary creation (closeout playmaking, secondary PnR, extra-pass)

Defensive job (POA, wing stopper, chaser, gambler, liability)

Physical form (micro guard, combo guard, big SG, power SG, etc.)

Overlays (analytics-optimized, neuro-trained subset, biomechanics-tuned, AI-scout-assisted, etc.)

So “all conceivable SG types” = a library of atomic SG roles you can combine, plus overlays that modify any role.

Atomic SG archetypes (role building blocks)
A) Off-ball shooting and gravity roles

SG value here is: spacing + movement + forcing defensive attention without dominating the ball.

1) Corner Sniper

Key characteristics: corner-only or corner-preferred shooter; low dribble; disciplined spacing.

Strengths: stretches help defense; punishes paint help; high efficiency looks.

Weaknesses: if run off the line, may be limited; can be “stationary.”

Best systems: drive-and-kick, 5-out, star heliocentric offenses.

Team dynamics: makes drives cleaner; teammates know exactly where he lives.

Example: Hypo: “never leaves corners, hits 45%.”

2) Above-the-Break Spot-Up Specialist

Key characteristics: catches on the wing/slot; fast prep feet; consistent release.

Strengths: scalable next to any creator; simple but valuable.

Weaknesses: low self-creation; can be neutralized if defenders top-lock and he can’t punish.

Best systems: spread PnR, pace-and-space, kickout-heavy teams.

Team dynamics: stabilizes spacing; keeps ball moving (catch-shoot-swing).

Example: Hypo: “slot sniper with no dribble game.”

3) Stationary Floor-Spacer

Key characteristics: prioritizes being “in the right spot” over motion; low turnover profile.

Strengths: reduces chaos; supports structured offenses.

Weaknesses: creates fewer defensive mistakes than movers; easier to pre-rotate against.

Best systems: set-heavy teams, inside-out post hubs, conservative offenses.

Team dynamics: clear lanes/corners; can make offense feel predictable.

Example: Hypo: “parks weakside, rarely relocates.”

4) Relocation Shooter (Drift/Lift/Replace)

Key characteristics: constantly re-spaces behind drives; reads defender head turns.

Strengths: turns good shots into great shots; punishes help-and-recover.

Weaknesses: requires chemistry; can over-move into teammates’ space.

Best systems: drive-and-kick, motion, read-and-react.

Team dynamics: forces teammates to keep eyes up; improves “second efforts” on offense.

Example: Hypo: “drifts to corner on every drive, always available.”

5) Transition Trail Shooter

Key characteristics: sprints to the arc in secondary break; quick-set feet; shoots early.

Strengths: punishes slow cross-matches; creates high-leverage early threes.

Weaknesses: if misses early, can feed opponent runouts; can ignore rim pressure opportunities.

Best systems: fast pace, rebound-and-run, deep bench teams.

Team dynamics: teammates push the ball knowing the trailer is a weapon.

Example: Hypo: “first three seconds = trail 3 threat.”

6) Quick-Release Trigger

Key characteristics: ultra-fast catch-to-shot; minimal dip; high reps shooting.

Strengths: shrinks closeout windows; makes “almost open” shots open.

Weaknesses: can be one-dimensional; if forced into dribbles, efficiency drops.

Best systems: any spacing offense; especially against aggressive help.

Team dynamics: creators trust tiny passing windows; defenders stay glued (opens lanes).

Example: Hypo: “0.3s release—shoots over late contests.”

7) Movement Shooter (Pindown/Stagger Runner)

Key characteristics: cardio, route-running, reads screens like a receiver; shoots on the move.

Strengths: forces chases and switches; bends defense even without dribbling.

Weaknesses: needs screeners and timing; can be denied physically.

Best systems: motion, Euro-style actions, floppy/stagger packages.

Team dynamics: teammates become screeners; offense gains constant motion.

Example: Hypo: “lives on staggers, never stops moving.”

8) Elevator/Stack Screen Specialist

Key characteristics: uses tight screen corridors; strong balance; quick square-up.

Strengths: creates instant high-quality looks; punishes ball-watching defenses.

Weaknesses: high dependence on precise screening; can be scouted.

Best systems: set-heavy offenses, ATO-heavy teams.

Team dynamics: role players buy into screening; creates “scripted points.”

Example: Hypo: “elevator action = automatic attempt.”

9) Flare/Exit Screen Specialist

Key characteristics: reads top-lock/deny; uses flares and exits to pop free.

Strengths: creates threes without needing the ball much; punishes denial.

Weaknesses: if defender fights through and no counters exist, can disappear.

Best systems: motion with flare packages; teams with smart screeners.

Team dynamics: increases cutting/screening IQ across roster.

Example: Hypo: “always available on weakside flare.”

10) DHO Sniper (Hand-Off Shooter)

Key characteristics: shoots off handoffs; tight footwork at the catch; can turn the corner.

Strengths: merges shooting + downhill threat; hard to guard with bigs involved.

Weaknesses: if handoff hub is weak, action dies; can be trapped.

Best systems: elbow hubs, 5-out DHOs, big playmaking cores.

Team dynamics: bigs become play hubs; SG becomes constant threat.

Example: Hypo: “handoff = instant pull or downhill.”

11) Curl Shooter (Catch into Midrange/3)

Key characteristics: uses curls off screens; can stop-and-pop or shoot in stride.

Strengths: punishes overplays; provides midrange counter in tight games.

Weaknesses: can drift into low-math shot diet if overused.

Best systems: screen-heavy motions, late-clock packages.

Team dynamics: defenders can’t top-lock freely; opens slips for screeners.

Example: Hypo: “curl to 15-foot pull-up on demand.”

12) Decoy / Gravity Runner

Key characteristics: runs hard off actions even when not getting the ball; accepts “weaponized movement.”

Strengths: creates openings for others; forces defensive communication fatigue.

Weaknesses: low box-score; can get frustrated; requires trust.

Best systems: motion offenses, star-centric teams that use decoys.

Team dynamics: teammates get cleaner looks; requires buy-in and humility.

Example: Hypo: “runs two screens every trip to free the star.”

13) Catch-to-Create “One-Dribble” Shooter

Key characteristics: attacks closeouts with 1–2 dribbles into pull-up or paint touch.

Strengths: prevents hard closeouts; keeps offense flowing without a primary handler.

Weaknesses: not a full creator; can be forced into tough shots if overextended.

Best systems: drive-and-kick, advantage basketball (touch advantage → attack).

Team dynamics: punishes rotations; encourages teammates to swing the ball.

Example: Hypo: “pump-fake → one dribble pull-up.”

14) Deep-Range Gravity SG

Key characteristics: credible far beyond line; demands high pickup even off-ball.

Strengths: expands spacing geometry; makes help defense expensive.

Weaknesses: variance; can be denied physically; needs stamina.

Best systems: 5-out, motion, early offense.

Team dynamics: lanes widen; defenders stay attached (less help).

Example: Hypo: “defender won’t leave him at 30 feet.”

15) “Old-School Set Shooter” Two-Guard

Key characteristics: prefers set feet, rhythm catches; less movement/relocation emphasis.

Strengths: reliable if fed correctly; fits structured eras/styles.

Weaknesses: struggles vs modern chase/deny unless adapted.

Best systems: set-play heavy, slower tempo, inside-out structure.

Team dynamics: teammates learn to deliver on-time passes and screens.

Example: Hypo: “needs the catch exactly in his pocket.”

B) On-ball scoring creators (SG as shot-maker)

SG value here is: self-generated points when structure breaks or defenses switch.

16) Pull-Up Three Bomber

Key characteristics: comfortable off-dribble from deep; quick trigger off screens.

Strengths: punishes drop/under; creates gravity without needing a big advantage.

Weaknesses: high variance; can hijack shot diet.

Best systems: spread PnR, early offense, drag screens.

Team dynamics: teammates space wider; bigs screen higher.

Example: Hypo: “walk-up pull-ups, 10 attempts/night.”

17) Midrange Pull-Up Artisan

Key characteristics: elite stop-start; pull-up from elbows/short corners; balance under contact.

Strengths: late-clock stabilizer; punishes switching and paint walls.

Weaknesses: can lower team 3/rim volume if overused.

Best systems: half-court heavy, playoff-style possessions.

Team dynamics: gives team a reliable bailout; may slow pace.

Example: Hypo: “lives at the elbows, automatic.”

18) Three-Level Scorer

Key characteristics: credible at rim/mid/3; reads defender leverage; can punish any coverage.

Strengths: hardest to scheme; keeps offense alive in any lineup.

Weaknesses: high workload; can drift into hero ball.

Best systems: any—especially when surrounded by spacing and simple reads.

Team dynamics: centralizes possessions; role players become spacers/cutters.

Example: Hypo: “scores efficiently from all three levels.”

19) Pure Isolation Package Scorer

Key characteristics: deep move library; counters; thrives 1v1.

Strengths: breaks switching defenses; creates shots from nothing.

Weaknesses: ball-stopping risk; teammates can become spectators.

Best systems: spread floors, switch-heavy environments.

Team dynamics: usage concentrates; off-ball discipline becomes critical.

Example: Hypo: “clears a side and cooks.”

20) Separation Creator (Step-Back / Side-Step)

Key characteristics: deceleration + lateral separation; shot creation vs tight defense.

Strengths: manufactures attempts late clock; punishes aggressive closeouts.

Weaknesses: difficult shot diet; cold nights are brutal.

Best systems: 5-out, switch-hunting, late-clock possessions.

Team dynamics: defenders play higher; opens backcuts for others.

Example: Hypo: “creates 3 feet on demand.”

21) Post-Up Bully Two-Guard

Key characteristics: strong base; backs down smaller guards; passes out of digs.

Strengths: punishes switches; creates doubles and inside-out threes.

Weaknesses: can slow tempo; spacing can clog if teammates don’t relocate.

Best systems: mismatch hunting, inside-out, physical leagues.

Team dynamics: wings cut/spot; bigs clear dunker space.

Example: Hypo: “SG who lives on the block.”

22) Mismatch Hunter (Screen-to-Switch Operator)

Key characteristics: calls actions specifically to get desired defender; patient manipulation.

Strengths: consistent advantage creation; playoff-friendly.

Weaknesses: can burn clock “hunting;” predictable if no counters.

Best systems: veteran teams, scout-heavy environments.

Team dynamics: teammates become screen tools; requires buy-in.

Example: Hypo: “runs 2–3 screens just to get the matchup.”

23) Tough-Shot Contest Maker

Key characteristics: comfort shooting over hands; high release points; footwork into difficult looks.

Strengths: survives elite defense; bails out broken possessions.

Weaknesses: efficiency can be mediocre; encourages bad habits.

Best systems: when spacing is weak or against elite defenses.

Team dynamics: teammates may stop moving expecting magic.

Example: Hypo: “hits shots that shouldn’t be available.”

24) Late-Clock Bailout SG

Key characteristics: calm under pressure; can generate a shot in 6–8 seconds.

Strengths: prevents empty trips; stabilizes close games.

Weaknesses: can enable lazy offense earlier in the clock.

Best systems: any team that needs an end-of-clock solution.

Team dynamics: teammates defer late; can reduce early-ball movement.

Example: Hypo: “6 seconds left = gets a look.”

25) Microwave Bench Scorer

Key characteristics: instant aggression; shots come fast; thrives vs second units.

Strengths: changes momentum; keeps scoring afloat without starters.

Weaknesses: defensive inconsistency; volatile shot quality.

Best systems: bench units needing a usage engine.

Team dynamics: raises bench ceiling; increases variance.

Example: Hypo: “enters and scores 10 in 4 minutes.”

26) Heat-Check High-Variance Gunner

Key characteristics: confidence-based; will take extreme difficulty shots when “hot.”

Strengths: can break games open; stretches defense psychologically.

Weaknesses: can sink efficiency quickly; hard to manage.

Best systems: underdog teams, fast pace, playground.

Team dynamics: emotional swings; teammates must rebound and stay steady.

Example: Hypo: “green light with no yellow.”

27) Rhythm-Dependent Volume Scorer

Key characteristics: needs early touches; performance swings with involvement.

Strengths: when fed, becomes a scoring engine.

Weaknesses: if ignored early, can disengage; shot forcing risk.

Best systems: coaches willing to script early looks.

Team dynamics: teammates adjust early actions to feed him.

Example: Hypo: “needs 2 early jumpers to unlock.”

28) Cold-Blood Closer SG

Key characteristics: late-game shot selection, composure, willingness to take the last shot.

Strengths: endgame edge; reduces team anxiety.

Weaknesses: can coast early; may overtake crunch-time possessions.

Best systems: teams in close games often; playoff-style.

Team dynamics: clear endgame hierarchy; others become spacers/screens.

Example: Hypo: “4th quarter assassin.”

29) “Catch, Jab, Cook” Triple-Threat Scorer

Key characteristics: elite jab series; attacks from wing without heavy dribble setup.

Strengths: quick scoring without slowing offense too much.

Weaknesses: if defender crowds and help sits, can be forced into tough twos.

Best systems: wing-oriented offenses; spread floors.

Team dynamics: reduces reliance on PG creation; increases wing isolation frequency.

Example: Hypo: “wing catch = immediate threat.”

30) Screen-Reject Shot Creator

Key characteristics: uses fake screens, rejects into open space; punishes defender anticipation.

Strengths: beats scouting; creates odd-angle drives/pull-ups.

Weaknesses: can over-dribble; timing dependent.

Best systems: teams that run lots of guard screening actions.

Team dynamics: teammates must stay spaced for sudden re-routes.

Example: Hypo: “never uses the screen the same way twice.”

C) Rim pressure, cutting, and finishing (SG scores without being the main handler)

SG value here is: efficient points from movement, closeouts, and transition.

31) Closeout Slasher (Straight-Line Driver)

Key characteristics: attacks hard closeouts; strong first step; simple reads.

Strengths: punishes rotations; generates rim attempts and kickouts.

Weaknesses: if defense stays home and he can’t create, limited.

Best systems: drive-and-kick, star gravity offenses.

Team dynamics: forces defenders to choose: help off him or give up drives.

Example: Hypo: “3-and-drive specialist.”

32) Change-of-Pace Driver

Key characteristics: hesitation, deceleration, stops; wins with timing.

Strengths: effective in traffic; draws help late.

Weaknesses: can settle into inefficient floaters if not disciplined.

Best systems: half-court heavy, physical defenses.

Team dynamics: cutting becomes timing-based around his pauses.

Example: Hypo: “slow-fast slasher.”

33) Contact Foul-Drawing Attacker

Key characteristics: seeks body contact; uses angles; strong at drawing whistles (or just contact points in pickup).

Strengths: stabilizes scoring; puts defenders in foul trouble.

Weaknesses: can be whistle-dependent; slows pace; can hurt flow.

Best systems: teams needing FT generation; late-game offense.

Team dynamics: opponents defend softer; teammates space for drives.

Example: Hypo: “lives at the line.”

34) Off-Screen Cutter-to-Rim (Curl-and-Go)

Key characteristics: uses screens to turn corner into paint; finishes or sprays out.

Strengths: combines movement with rim pressure; hard to top-lock.

Weaknesses: needs screeners and timing; can be bumped off routes.

Best systems: motion, screen-heavy sets, Euro actions.

Team dynamics: screeners get rewarded; creates help rotations.

Example: Hypo: “curl = rim touch.”

35) Backdoor Cutter Specialist

Key characteristics: elite at reading overplays/denials; quick decisions at rim.

Strengths: punishes aggressive defense; creates layups without dribbles.

Weaknesses: needs passers who see it; can be crowded in poor spacing.

Best systems: Princeton-style, split cuts, motion.

Team dynamics: defenders can’t overplay; opens perimeter shooting.

Example: Hypo: “top-locked? automatic backdoor.”

36) Baseline “Hammer” Cutter

Key characteristics: times baseline cuts behind help; finishes or becomes corner outlet.

Strengths: punishes help rotations; creates either dunks or corner threes.

Weaknesses: requires great timing and spacing; can be neutralized by disciplined weakside defense.

Best systems: drive-heavy offenses with strong corner spacing principles.

Team dynamics: forces weakside defenders to stay honest; increases corner opportunities.

Example: Hypo: “baseline ghost that appears for layups.”

37) Transition Lane Runner Finisher

Key characteristics: sprints wide lanes; attacks rim in early offense; minimal dribble.

Strengths: easy points; constant pressure on cross-matches.

Weaknesses: if game slows, impact can drop.

Best systems: fast pace, rebound-and-run, pressing teams.

Team dynamics: teammates push the ball knowing lanes will be filled.

Example: Hypo: “first wing down the floor every time.”

38) Above-the-Rim Alley Finisher SG

Key characteristics: vertical pop; cuts and catches lobs; fearless in traffic.

Strengths: turns small advantages into dunks; forces weakside tags.

Weaknesses: if not a shooter, can be sagged off; injury risk.

Best systems: PnR + weakside lob threats, athletic rosters.

Team dynamics: defenders tag earlier; shooters get cleaner looks.

Example: Hypo: “SG who’s basically a wing lob threat.”

39) In-Between Floater Specialist

Key characteristics: elite runner/floater; soft touch; uses gaps vs rim protectors.

Strengths: counters drop and big rim protectors; efficient in tight paint.

Weaknesses: can become a crutch; limits rim pressure if overused.

Best systems: PnR second-side attacks, congested half-court.

Team dynamics: reduces turnovers in traffic; keeps scoring alive vs size.

Example: Hypo: “automatic 6–10 foot runner.”

40) Non-Shooter Slasher (Spacing Tax Two-Guard)

Key characteristics: minimal 3 threat; wins via cuts, drives, transition.

Strengths: can still generate paint pressure and passing if elite athlete/IQ.

Weaknesses: defenders help off; shrinks spacing; hard to pair with non-shoot bigs.

Best systems: lineups with 3–4 shooters around him; heavy cutting.

Team dynamics: teammates must shoot to keep floor open; offense becomes more binary.

Example: Hypo: “can’t shoot but collapses the paint.”

41) Offensive Rebound Crasher Guard

Key characteristics: hunts long rebounds; times crashes from corner/wing.

Strengths: creates extra possessions; punishes teams that don’t box guards.

Weaknesses: can hurt transition defense; can clog spacing if mistimed.

Best systems: teams emphasizing OREB, physical identity.

Team dynamics: others must cover back; increases second-chance points.

Example: Hypo: “SG who grabs 3–4 OREBs.”

42) “Short-Corner / Dunker Spot” Guard (Unconventional)

Key characteristics: plays like a small wing/big on offense; lurks baseline for dumps and cuts.

Strengths: can exploit over-help; adds cutting pressure.

Weaknesses: if defender ignores him, spacing suffers; role is niche.

Best systems: heavy drive teams that want baseline finishers.

Team dynamics: changes normal guard spacing rules; forces specific lineup fits.

Example: Hypo: “guard who lives in dunker spot.”

D) Playmaking and connector SG roles (secondary creation)

SG value here is: keeping advantage alive without needing to be “the point.”

43) Secondary PnR Operator

Key characteristics: can run ball screens when PG sits or is denied; solid reads.

Strengths: prevents predictability; punishes traps on primary handler.

Weaknesses: not always elite at controlling tempo; turnover risk under pressure.

Best systems: dual-creator lineups, staggered rotations.

Team dynamics: reduces load on PG; creates multiple initiation points.

Example: Hypo: “two-guard who runs 8 PnRs a game.”

44) Inverted PnR Receiver (PG as Screener)

Key characteristics: uses smaller guard screens to create downhill lanes or switches.

Strengths: breaks switching rules; generates confusion advantages.

Weaknesses: if screener isn’t respected, defenders ignore the screen.

Best systems: positionless offenses, switch-heavy matchups.

Team dynamics: forces shared screening; roles blur.

Example: Hypo: “SG gets a guard screen and attacks bigs.”

45) Second-Side Closeout Playmaker

Key characteristics: catches after initial rotation; attacks advantage; makes the next pass.

Strengths: turns first advantage into layups/corner threes.

Weaknesses: if hesitant, advantage dies; needs processing speed.

Best systems: drive-and-kick, star gravity offenses.

Team dynamics: rewards ball movement; teammates cut harder.

Example: Hypo: “catch on swing → drive → spray.”

46) Drive-and-Kick Connector

Key characteristics: not primary engine, but consistently gets paint touches and passes out.

Strengths: raises team 3PA quality; keeps defense rotating.

Weaknesses: if finishing is poor, defenders stop helping.

Best systems: spaced lineups, motion into advantage.

Team dynamics: makes role players relevant; encourages spacing discipline.

Example: Hypo: “paint touch machine from the wing.”

47) 0.5 Extra-Pass SG

Key characteristics: instant decisions; touch passes; never over-holds.

Strengths: increases pace of execution; creates “hockey assists.”

Weaknesses: can disappear if not assertive; relies on teammates converting.

Best systems: motion, read-and-react, high-IQ teams.

Team dynamics: everyone touches; offense feels connected.

Example: Hypo: “rarely dribbles more than once.”

48) Post-Entry / Inside-Out Feeder

Key characteristics: wing entry angles, timing, disguise; feeds post without turnovers.

Strengths: unlocks post hubs; starts inside-out offense.

Weaknesses: if post play is weak, value shrinks.

Best systems: post hubs, split action, inside-out teams.

Team dynamics: bigs feel empowered; cutters learn timing off doubles.

Example: Hypo: “wing that always gets the entry in.”

49) Transition Hit-Ahead / Advance Passer

Key characteristics: pushes pace with early passing; finds corners/wings ahead.

Strengths: creates transition threes/layups without dribbling.

Weaknesses: turnovers if lanes aren’t filled; requires runners.

Best systems: high-pace teams, wings who sprint, early offense.

Team dynamics: wings run harder; break becomes structured.

Example: Hypo: “catches outlet, immediately throws ahead.”

50) DHO Playmaker (Handoff into Pass)

Key characteristics: uses handoffs to turn corner, then hits rollers/corners.

Strengths: merges scoring threat with passing reads; tough to trap.

Weaknesses: if his jumper isn’t respected, handoff defense sits.

Best systems: elbow hub offenses, 5-out DHOs.

Team dynamics: bigs get easy assists; cutters get layups.

Example: Hypo: “handoff turns into pocket pass.”

51) Zone-Buster Gap Flash Playmaker

Key characteristics: finds soft spots, catches at nail/short corner, delivers quick reads.

Strengths: punishes zones and packed paints; creates high-percentage looks.

Weaknesses: if he can’t shoot/finish from gaps, defenses collapse.

Best systems: teams facing zone/pack principles; high IQ lineups.

Team dynamics: improves spacing geometry; teaches teammates to cut behind zones.

Example: Hypo: “nail catcher who instantly hits baseline cutter.”

52) Guard-as-Screener Short-Roll Decision Maker

Key characteristics: sets screens, then receives short roll like a mini-big; makes 4v3 reads.

Strengths: destroys switches; creates advantage without needing elite dribble.

Weaknesses: physical toll; if teammates can’t space, reads vanish.

Best systems: positionless, motion, switch-heavy matchups.

Team dynamics: everyone screens; offense becomes more interconnected.

Example: Hypo: “SG sets screen, catches at foul line, plays QB.”

E) Defense-first SG roles

SG value here is: who you can guard and how you shape the perimeter scheme.

53) Point-of-Attack Stopper Two-Guard

Key characteristics: guards primary ball handler; contains drives; disciplined hands.

Strengths: raises defensive ceiling; prevents collapse before it starts.

Weaknesses: offensive burden may need to be low to sustain effort.

Best systems: any defense; especially contain/drop schemes needing strong POA.

Team dynamics: bigs defend simpler; team stays out of rotation.

Example: Hypo: “takes the toughest guard assignment nightly.”

54) Wing Stopper Two-Guard

Key characteristics: guards scorers 2–3 (sometimes 4); strong chest; contests without fouling.

Strengths: solves matchup problems; enables smaller lineups offensively.

Weaknesses: may struggle chasing tiny speed guards.

Best systems: switch-heavy, playoff matchups.

Team dynamics: allows hiding weaker defenders elsewhere.

Example: Hypo: “SG who guards the star wing.”

55) Screen Chaser (Shooter-Tracker)

Key characteristics: elite at navigating off-ball screens; never loses contact.

Strengths: neutralizes movement shooters; reduces defensive breakdowns.

Weaknesses: tiring; can be screened repeatedly into fatigue.

Best systems: teams facing lots of motion/off-screen offenses.

Team dynamics: keeps defense intact; reduces panic rotations.

Example: Hypo: “sticks to shooters through mazes.”

56) Denial / Top-Lock Specialist

Key characteristics: denies catches; top-locks; forces backdoors into help.

Strengths: disrupts offense before it starts; forces timing errors.

Weaknesses: backdoor risk if team help isn’t sharp.

Best systems: aggressive denial teams; high-IQ defenses.

Team dynamics: everyone must rotate correctly behind denial pressure.

Example: Hypo: “makes elite shooters work just to touch it.”

57) Switchable Multi-Position Defender

Key characteristics: guards 1–3/4; strong hips and core; survives in post.

Strengths: enables switch-everything; simplifies defensive rules.

Weaknesses: may sacrifice quickness vs micro guards.

Best systems: small ball, positionless defenses.

Team dynamics: lineup flexibility increases; fewer weak links to hunt.

Example: Hypo: “guards whoever is hot.”

58) Help-and-Recover Rotations Ace

Key characteristics: perfect stunt timing, digs, nail help, x-outs; recovers to shooters.

Strengths: makes defense feel “bigger;” reduces corner threes and layups.

Weaknesses: requires high conditioning and IQ; small mistakes become open shots.

Best systems: help-heavy schemes, aggressive nail defenses.

Team dynamics: teammates trust help; can pressure ball more.

Example: Hypo: “always in the right help window.”

59) Ballhawk Gambler

Key characteristics: jumps lanes, digs hard, creates steals for transition.

Strengths: creates extra possessions and momentum.

Weaknesses: gives up structure; can cause blow-bys and rotations.

Best systems: teams with strong backline rim protection and cover length.

Team dynamics: high variance; others must cover his gambles.

Example: Hypo: “2 steals and 2 breakdowns per quarter.”

60) Full-Court Pest Two-Guard

Key characteristics: picks up early, pressures dribble, disrupts timing.

Strengths: speeds opponents up; creates fatigue and errors.

Weaknesses: foul trouble; can be beaten by calm ball movement.

Best systems: pressing/tempo teams, deep benches.

Team dynamics: increases pace; demands team-wide rotation behind pressure.

Example: Hypo: “turns games into cardio fights.”

61) Physical Tone-Setter / Bulldog

Key characteristics: embraces contact; fights through screens; sets emotional edge.

Strengths: disrupts finesse; makes every catch uncomfortable.

Weaknesses: foul/tech risk; can be exploited by speed if over-physical.

Best systems: grinder defenses, physical leagues.

Team dynamics: raises toughness; can raise foul volatility too.

Example: Hypo: “wins possessions with force.”

62) Defensive Liability Two-Guard (Target)

Key characteristics: poor containment/screen navigation; low awareness or effort.

Strengths: often paired with elite shooting/scoring to justify minutes.

Weaknesses: hunted relentlessly; forces help; burns teammates’ energy.

Best systems: teams that can hide defenders (zone principles, elite rim protection).

Team dynamics: teammates over-rotate; transition defense suffers.

Example: Hypo: “offense star, defense target.”

F) Size and positional hybrids (who “plays SG”)
63) Combo Guard (SG/PG Hybrid)

Key characteristics: can initiate sometimes; can play off ball; flexible usage.

Strengths: lineup versatility; helps vs pressure and traps.

Weaknesses: role ambiguity; may be average at both instead of elite at one.

Best systems: dual-initiator teams, staggered creators.

Team dynamics: reduces reliance on a single ball handler.

Example: Hypo: “not a pure PG, but can run offense in stretches.”

64) Point SG (Primary Initiator Two-Guard)

Key characteristics: SG by scoring mentality, but handles primary creation.

Strengths: scoring pressure + playmaking; hard to scheme if he shoots.

Weaknesses: turnover risk; can drift into score-first tunnel vision.

Best systems: heliocentric, spread PnR, 5-out.

Team dynamics: traditional PG becomes off-ball spacer/defender.

Example: Hypo: “SG who runs 40 PnRs a game.”

65) Big SG / Small Wing Two-Guard

Key characteristics: 6'6–6'9 style; shoots over smaller guards; can defend wings.

Strengths: mismatch creation; switchability; rebounding advantage.

Weaknesses: may struggle vs pressure from tiny fast defenders.

Best systems: positionless, switch-heavy, spacing lineups.

Team dynamics: changes matchup map; allows smaller guards elsewhere.

Example: Hypo: “wing-sized SG who shoots over contests.”

66) Micro SG (Tiny Scoring Two-Guard)

Key characteristics: very small; elite quickness and handle; often high-variance scorer.

Strengths: breaks pressure; creates separation; can dominate loose defenses.

Weaknesses: defensive target; finishing over size; requires protection.

Best systems: high pace, spacing, help-heavy defenses behind him.

Team dynamics: teammates must cover defense; offense gets speed and chaos.

Example: Hypo: “tiny flamethrower off screens.”

67) Power Two-Guard (Strength-Based SG)

Key characteristics: strong frame; downhill contact; post seals; physical defense.

Strengths: punishes switches; draws help; sets physical tone.

Weaknesses: if jumper is weak, spacing issues appear.

Best systems: physical leagues, switch-hunting, small-ball.

Team dynamics: teammates spot and cut; defense collapses earlier.

Example: Hypo: “bully SG who lives at the rim and line.”

68) Rebounding SG (Board-Impact Two-Guard)

Key characteristics: crashes defensive boards; starts breaks; strong box-out instincts.

Stackable overlays (subsets that modify ANY SG archetype)

These are not base SG types. They’re modifiers you can apply to any archetype above (including neuro-training as a subset).

Overlay 1) Neuro-Trained Processing Subset

Faster perception/decision under stress and fatigue; better shot/no-shot inhibition.

Impact: improves shot selection, closeout reads, and defensive rotations without changing “role.”

Overlay 2) Biomechanics-Tuned Shooting Form

Release consistency, lower-body alignment, repeatable off-movement mechanics.

Impact: turns “almost shooters” into real spacers; improves movement-shot accuracy.

Overlay 3) Fatigue-Resistant Shooting Conditioning

Motor patterns hold late; legs don’t die in 4Q or long runs.

Impact: makes movement shooters and trail shooters sustainable.

Overlay 4) AI-Assisted Scouting Translation

Faster internalization of opponent closeouts, top-lock rules, and chase coverages.

Impact: better counter selection (backdoor, curl, fade, slip) in real time.

Overlay 5) Analytics-Optimized Shot Map Rules

Disciplined “rim/3/FT first,” with situational midrange permission.

Impact: reduces empty long twos and early contested shots.

Overlay 6) Contact-Tolerance / Landing-Control Training

Safer finishes and jump-shot landings; fewer small injuries.

Impact: increases availability and confidence taking contested threes or finishing through bodies.

Overlay 7) Ambidextrous Finishing and Passing

Can finish and pass with either hand on the move.

Impact: makes closeouts and drives less steerable; reduces turnovers.

Overlay 8) Screen-Running Route Optimization

SG learns optimal angles, pacing, and “setups” to get defenders stuck.

Impact: movement archetypes become more efficient with fewer possessions wasted.

Overlay 9) Defensive Technique Optimization

Hip mobility, screen navigation mechanics, hand discipline.

Impact: upgrades POA/chaser roles and reduces foul-prone behaviors.

Overlay 10) High-Variance “Chaos Preference”

Embraces volatility: early threes, risky steals, fast attacks.

Impact: raises upset potential; raises inconsistency and team stress.
